Eisenhower Matrix apps for Android: what to compare

17 November 2026

Search Google Play for "Eisenhower Matrix" and you get a long list of apps that all show four coloured boxes. The boxes are the easy part. What decides whether you still use the app in a month is everything around them.

Full disclosure: we make one of these apps, Eisenhower Matrix: To-Do List. This guide is meant to help whichever app you choose, so it lists the questions, not a winner.

Three kinds of app

  • Dedicated matrix apps. The four boxes are the whole app. Usually simple and quick, and often free with an optional upgrade.
  • Big to-do apps with a matrix view. Some large task managers, TickTick for example, include a four-quadrant view inside a much bigger app. Good if you already live in that app; usually a subscription for the full set.
  • Templates. A Notion page, a spreadsheet or a printable. Flexible and free, but nothing reminds you, and nothing sorts tasks for you.

Eight things to compare

1. How a task gets into a box

Do you drag tasks into boxes, or does the app ask "urgent?" and "important?" and file the task for you? Asking the two questions every time is the habit the method is built on.

2. How fast you can add a task

The task you never write down is the one you forget. Look for a home screen widget, voice entry, or a way to add a task without opening the app at all.

3. What happens to yesterday's tasks

Any list gets stale. Check whether the app helps you go back through overdue and due-today tasks, or whether they just pile up in red.

4. Dates, reminders and repeats

Schedule-box tasks need a date to ever happen. Check for due dates, reminders that actually fire, and recurring tasks for the weekly or monthly jobs.

5. Where your tasks are stored

Does the app need an account? Are tasks kept on your phone or on the developer's server? Read the privacy policy, and look at the Data safety section on the Play listing.

6. How you get your tasks out

If you change phones or apps, can you export your tasks to a file and import them again? Backups matter more than they seem until the day you need one.

7. The price model

Free with ads, a subscription, or a one-time upgrade? Check which features are actually free. A matrix app that charges a monthly fee for the core four boxes is worth questioning.

8. Your language and your screen

Is the app in your language? Does it work in landscape and on a tablet? Does it respect large text and dark mode?

Whatever you choose, try it for a full week with your real list. The right app is the one you still open on day eight.
