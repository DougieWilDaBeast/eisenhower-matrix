A New Year plan that lasts past January

19 November 2026

Most New Year plans don't fail on 1 January. They fail in the third week, when last year's unfinished jobs, this week's emergencies and the big new goals all land on the same list. The Eisenhower Matrix won't give you more hours, but it makes it obvious which of them each task deserves.

Step 1: clear the backlog before January

Don't carry 40 overdue tasks into a fresh year. In the last two weeks of December, take 15 minutes and give every old task one answer: done, today, another day (with a real date) or delete. If something has been overdue since October and nothing bad happened, delete it on purpose. The full method is in how to clear an overdue task list.

Step 2: choose what matters this week

A goal for the year is too big to act on. A box for this week isn't. On the first Monday of January, sort your list into the four boxes:

Do
  • Urgent and important
  • Three tasks at most for the week
Schedule
  • Important, not urgent
  • This is where New Year goals live: give one a date this week
Delegate
  • Urgent, not important to you
  • Ask, hand over, or answer in one batch
Backlog
  • Neither
  • Park it; look again next month

The trick is the Schedule box. "Get fit" is a wish; "Tuesday, 7 am, 20-minute walk" is a task with a date, and dated tasks get done. Need help deciding what's urgent? See ten tasks people sort wrongly.

Step 3: keep it going after the motivation fades

  • Two minutes a day. Each morning, look at today's and overdue tasks and decide each one again. This is what stops a backlog growing back.
  • Ten minutes on Sunday. Empty the Do box, date the next step of one Schedule goal, and delete one Backlog task you'll never do.
  • Repeat the boring jobs automatically. Bins, bills and the weekly shop are recurring tasks, not things to remember.
  • Expect a bad week. When it comes, do step 1 again for 15 minutes and carry on. One clear-out beats a perfect streak.
