How to clear an overdue task list

17 November 2026

An overdue list is not a plan. It's a record of every day the plan didn't survive. The fix isn't more willpower; it's making one small decision about each task, then making it again tomorrow.

Why overdue lists grow

  • Dates were guesses. "Friday" was a hope, not a commitment.
  • Some tasks were never important. They were urgent at the time, or just felt like they should be done.
  • Nothing asked you to decide. A task with a passed date just turns red and waits.

The 15-minute clear-out

Set a timer. Go through the overdue tasks one at a time, oldest first, and give each one exactly one of four answers:

Done
  • Already did it and never ticked it off
  • Takes under two minutes: do it now
Today
  • Really urgent and really important
  • At most three of these
Another day
  • Important, not urgent
  • Pick a real date you'll keep
Delete
  • Neither, any more
  • Let it go on purpose

Two questions settle most tasks: what happens if this waits a week? If nothing, it isn't urgent. Would I be glad in a year that I did it? If not, it probably isn't important. That's the Eisenhower Matrix in two sentences; see urgent vs important for ten tasks people sort wrongly.

Rules that stop it backfiring

  • No more than three "today" answers. Moving everything to today only builds tomorrow's overdue list.
  • Dates a few days ahead, not tomorrow. Spread them out so next week isn't a wall.
  • Deleting is allowed. If it has been overdue for a month and nothing bad happened, that's your answer.
  • Stop when the timer ends. Do the rest tomorrow. The habit matters more than one perfect clear-out.

Make it a two-minute daily habit

Once the backlog is gone, the same four decisions take a minute or two a day.
